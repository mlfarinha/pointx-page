Abstract

The appearance of a scene is determined by the albedo, roughness, metallicity and normal of every point on its surface, and by the light that reaches it, its irradiance. Recovering these intrinsics from images is ambiguous, and methods that predict them per pixel give the same surface point a different decomposition in every view. We introduce PointX, a simple feed-forward model that predicts intrinsics per 3D surface point instead. A multi-view transformer encodes a set of unposed images, and for any queried surface point, PointX pools the point's tokens across the views in which it appears and decodes the point's intrinsics once. Each point of a 3D reconstruction of the scene, such as a point cloud or a mesh, thus receives a single prediction, which is exactly consistent across views by construction and enables view-consistent relighting and editing. The same model also predicts per-view maps, from one image or many, by querying the point seen at every pixel. These maps agree across views up to the precision of the estimated geometry and correspondences. PointX is significantly more consistent than existing methods, while remaining competitive in accuracy from a single image and improving as views are added.

Method

Predict the point, not the pixel

Intrinsics are properties of surface points, so the surface point is the unit of prediction. A query names a 3D point; every view in which it appears contributes one token; the point is decoded once.

01

Locate the point in the views

The track of a point p is where it projects in each view. Projection also lands where p is hidden, so each location carries a confidence that p is visible there, from the distance between p and the surface that view images at that pixel.

cv=11+δv/τ,δv=‖Xv[xv]−p‖dv

Cameras and depth come from a frozen reconstruction model, a scan, or any other source. A dense matcher can supply the track instead.

02

Pool one token per view

Every view contributes the token at its location on the track, and the tokens are averaged with their confidences as weights. Occluded views count for little. The weights have no learned parameters and no view is the reference.

z=∑vwvtv[xv],wv=cv∑ucu

The pooled token depends only on the point, the geometry and the images, not on the view that asks.

03

Decode the point once

Five small MLPs read albedo, irradiance, roughness, metallicity and the normal from the pooled token. The answer belongs to the point: its disagreement across views is zero by construction, with no consistency loss and no fusion of per-view predictions.

Querying the points of a point cloud or the texels of a mesh puts intrinsics directly on that representation. Querying the point seen at every pixel gives per-view maps, from one image or many.

Benchmarks

Accurate and consistent

Per-view predictions trade accuracy against agreement between views. Decoding each surface point once removes the trade: PointX is the most consistent method and stays among the most accurate.

Applications

Relight it, edit it, once

One set of intrinsics per surface point attaches directly to a 3D representation, with no per-view maps to fuse. A light placed once or a material edited once changes every view consistently. Below, a bulb is hung in ETH3D scenes and the photographs are relit by it: pick a light and a view, and drag the divider between the photograph and its relit version.

Path traced in Blender, with every predicted map. The surface point at each pixel is queried once, from all the views in which it appears. The bulb is placed once in 3D and its light is path traced in Cycles on a surface that carries PointX's albedo, roughness, metallicity and normals as a standard physically based material, so it casts shadows, leaves highlights and bounces between surfaces.
